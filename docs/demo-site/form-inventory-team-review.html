Form Inventory — What We Found & What It Means

Full HDPM document library (OneDrive) · reviewed 7/22/2026 · for team discussion HIGH DESERT PROPERTY MANAGEMENT

The question we asked: can every form be pre-filled from AppFolio, completed by the right person, and filed back into AppFolio when done — no matter the process? The answer after inventorying both folders: yes, and it's better than that. About a third of our "forms" stop being forms at all — they become documents the system writes for us. The goal of this project is simple: less retyping, less folder-walking, less monotony — more time for owners, tenants, and judgment calls.

The five species (every form is one of these)

~13

1 · DATA-CAPTURE → becomes a tracked process

Forms that exist to collect fields as work moves between desks. These become live "jackets" — prefilled, on-screen, visible on every desk at once. The paper survives only as a PDF filed to AppFolio at the end.

Vacancy Tracking (Gold) · New/Acquired/Co-Tenant Set-Up · New Tenant Checklist · Remaining Tenant Screening · Key Pickups · Owner Information · Owner Close Out List · Owner Set-Up Checklist · Change of Ownership
Win: no more retyping tenant/unit/rent data that AppFolio already knows — it arrives filled in.
~10+

2 · GENERATED — nobody fills anything

Documents that are 100% derivable from data + a template. The system writes them; a human approves and sends.

Employment & rental verification requests (the 110-file folders!) · Application denial letters + FCRA · Delinquency reports (11 spreadsheets/yr) · Monthly inspection schedules · Owner Close Out letter · Move-out confirmations · Seasonal tenant communications
Win: the two "Can Delete" verification folders — 110 per-company copies of one letter — become 1 template + a contact list. That whole species of chore disappears.
~12

3 · SIGNATURE — prefill, then e-sign

Agreements needing tenant/owner signatures. Prefilled by the system, routed for e-signature (or print-sign-scan as fallback), auto-filed on completion.

Payment Agreements (4 variants → 1 route with a frequency setting) · One-Time Late/Partial Payment · Deposit to Hold · Utility Billing Addendum · Reasonable Accommodation · Owner Agency Agreement · Owner Approval of Applicant · Direct Deposit Auth · 30-Day Notice (resident form)
Win: Owner Approval of Applicant becomes a one-tap approval on the owner's phone — hours off every application.
~4

4 · INSPECTION — mobile capture with photos

Walk-around forms. Phone-first: tap through rooms, attach photos, PDF report generates itself and files to the unit page.

New Tenant Inspection · Owner Property Inspection Sheet · Cleaner Check-Off List · Move-Out Inspection
Win: the report writes itself in the driveway, not back at the desk.
~10

5 · THIRD-PARTY — fill & attach

Forms whose format someone else owns. We prefill what we can onto their PDF, a human completes the rest, and it files to the right page. The format stays theirs.

HUD 5380/5382 · Golden Key (RFTA) packet · HAP contract · Housing Works change-of-ownership · Landlord utility agreements (Cascade, Pacific Power, Bend, Redmond) · W-9 · TDS authorization
Win: name, address, and owner details land on the form before anyone opens it.
rest

NOT FORMS — knowledge & one-offs

ESA/HUD guidance, NSPIRE prep, violation-writing guide → these belong in the team knowledge base (askable in chat, not stored in folders). Commercial leases are bespoke legal documents — a later chapter (document assembly), deliberately out of scope now.

What this means for each desk

Front DeskNo more copying names/addresses onto set-up forms and vacancy sheets — they arrive prefilled. Key pickups logged in two taps. The 30-Day Notice Box becomes a queue on screen.
Property ManagersApprovals and agreements go out prefilled and come back signed and filed. The folder that bounced to your box now sits open on every desk that needs it, with the next action and date always visible.
MaintenanceInspections happen on the phone with photos; the Gold card's VACANCY section fills itself as you go; key counts check against the New Tenant Checklist automatically.
AccountingDelinquency workbooks stop being hand-built — a live view replaces 11 spreadsheets a year. Fees, holdover rent, and close-out charges arrive as queued items, not sticky notes.

Already live in hdpm-chat (this isn't starting from zero)

  • Key Manager (shipped 7/20) — replaces Z-Key List MASTER: permanent key numbers, assignment history that's never overwritten, AppFolio sync with a strict ownership split, spreadsheet import, stats. The Returned Keys / Key Pickup routes have their database waiting.
  • Maintenance OS foundations — work-order routes, vendor chase SMS, estimate-chaser escalation, AI triage proposals, digest recipients. The tripwire pattern is already running in production.
  • AppFolio webhook log + sync infrastructure — the event spine every route in this report plugs into.
  • Inspections tables (Mar 2026) — species 4 has its schema started.
  • Advertising tooling — Craigslist pages, cached vacancies, saved listings, lead events: much of the ADVERTISING track exists.
  • Staff registry, dashboards, KPI/metrics snapshots, reports, agent proposal outbox — the desks, the TV-board bones, and the notification plumbing.
  • Notion Knowledge Base — all 43 SOPs migrated (June 2026), plus roles/duties dashboard, personnel handbook, screening policy + decision guide, animal policy, fees configuration, and FAQ database. The knowledge layer exists; the engine links to it rather than duplicating it.
  • Digital Forms Project plan (Notion, Jan 2026) — this initiative's ancestor, with decisions still standing: SurveyJS selected as the form renderer (MIT, JSON definitions in Supabase — a natural fit for rendering jacket tracks), and the honest ROI math this report inherits: 25 new tenant setups/month × 65–95 min each ≈ 31 hrs/month on one process alone. Its copy/paste AppFolio workaround has since been superseded by the guided-step + verify-by-read pattern and the write-path decision teed up for Sep 4.

What this means: the engine work ahead is mostly the jacket/route layer (tracks, boxes, form rendering, e-sign, auto-filing) on top of plumbing that already exists — not a greenfield build.

Housekeeping found along the way

  • Widespread .doc/.docx/.pdf/.pub duplicates of the same form, plus ".old" files — the inventory above counts each form once; a cleanup pass would cut both folders roughly in half.
  • Four forms still exist only as Publisher (.pub) files — already on the modernization path via the branded redesigns.
  • Six move-in forms are already rebuilt in the new brand system — that redesign work becomes the on-screen look of the digital versions.
  • ESA/HUD guidance, NSPIRE prep, and violation-writing references aren't forms — they belong in a searchable team knowledge base.
  • Commercial leases are bespoke legal documents — deliberately out of scope for the engine (a later document-assembly chapter, if ever).

Questions for the team

1. Which form eats the most of your week? (Our guess from the data: verification letters, delinquency workbooks, and the Gold card — but you know better.)
2. The species table calls some forms "generated — nobody fills anything." What did we miss — where does human judgment actually live in those?
3. Whose box does each Gold-card section really belong to? (We inferred owners from the form; the table needs your corrections.)
4. If we could digitize only one process first, which would you pick — and would you run it side-by-side with paper for two weeks to prove it?
5. What's on paper that isn't in these folders at all?
6. The folder colors — yellow, red, green (and any others): what does each color mean today, and who decides which color a packet gets? (The digital version will keep the color code — a screen of jackets should read like the shelf does.)
7. What gets handwritten on the backs of forms? (We know work orders land on the back of the Gold sheet — what else lives in the margins? Whatever it is, the digital jacket needs a home for it or paper survives.)
